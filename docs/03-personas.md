# 03 — User Personas: Common-Man Focused

## Persona 1: Maria — The Busy Parent

**Demographics**: 38-year-old mother of three, suburban Ohio, household income $72K, works as a dental hygienist

### Goals
- Clear out kids' outgrown clothing, toys, and gear
- Make enough money to fund the next round of purchases
- Spend as little time as possible on the process

### Pain Points
- Has a garage full of items worth $800–$1,200 but has listed maybe 5% of it
- Tried Facebook Marketplace twice; once got a no-show, once got a lowball offer that offended her
- Doesn't know what anything is worth; prices randomly and either undersells or gets no interest
- Has no time — between work, school pickups, and activities, she has maybe 20 free minutes a day
- Worries about meeting strangers for pickup

### Behaviors
- Primary phone user (iPhone 14)
- Shops on Amazon, occasionally Target
- Has never used eBay; tried OfferUp once
- Communicates via text and iMessage
- Takes photos of everything with her phone camera

### How Her Agent Helps
Maria takes a photo of a Pottery Barn Kids dresser. Her agent identifies it as a 6-drawer dresser (model: Kendall), checks sold comps on eBay and Facebook Marketplace, writes a compelling listing with accurate dimensions and condition notes, sets the price at $185 (market rate: $150–$220), and posts it. When a buyer agent offers $155, Maria's agent counters at $175 and closes at $168 — all without Maria touching her phone. Maria receives a payment notification and arranges a contactless pickup through the app.

### What Success Looks Like
Maria clears her garage in 3 weekends without writing a single listing. She earns $900 from items she would have otherwise donated. She never negotiates with anyone. She tells her friends, "I just took photos and got money."

## Persona 2: Jamal — The College Student

### Behaviors
- Heavy social media user (Instagram, TikTok, Discord)
- Researches purchases extensively on YouTube but doesn't know where to buy
- Prefers messaging to phone calls
- Wants transactions that feel safe

### How His Agent Helps
Jamal tells his agent: "I need a good laptop for college. Budget is $350 max. Needs to run Microsoft Office and Zoom well. Prefer Dell or Lenovo. Used is fine if it works."

His agent finds 12 matching listings, filters to 4 that are within 15 miles, have sellers with 4.5+ trust scores, and are priced below market rate. The agent negotiates with the best option's seller agent, gets the price from $375 to $335, and presents Jamal with: "Found a Lenovo ThinkPad T14 (2022) in good condition. Seller has 47 transactions, 4.8 stars. Negotiated from $375 → $335. Ready to buy?" Jamal taps "Approve."

### What Success Looks Like
Jamal has a working laptop within 48 hours of searching, paid $65 below retail, never negotiated, and had zero anxiety about the transaction.

## Persona 3: Dave — The Tradesman

**Demographics**: 51-year-old electrician in Dallas, TX, selling tools and equipment after semi-retirement, has 30+ years of accumulated tools

### Goals
- Sell $5,000–$8,000 worth of specialized electrical tools
- Get fair market value — not garage sale prices
- Find buyers who understand what they're buying

### Pain Points
- Tools have model numbers and specs that are hard to describe without industry knowledge
- Doesn't know how to photograph electrical tools to show their condition
- Is uncomfortable with technology; uses his phone primarily for calls and texts
- Worried about tools going to people who won't use them properly (safety concern)
- Has listed items before but got questions he couldn't answer via text

### Behaviors
- Uses phone for calls primarily
- Would use voice input if available
- Trusts recommendations from people he knows
- Prefers cash or simple payment; suspicious of "fancy" payment systems

### How His Agent Helps
Dave says into his phone: "I want to sell my Klein Tools VDV501-851 tester kit, barely used." His agent recognizes the product, finds it sells for $45–$75 used, writes a detailed listing including all kit contents, condition notes, and relevant certifications, and posts it. The agent targets buyers with purchase history in electrical tools. When a buyer's agent asks if it works with fiber cables, Dave's agent correctly answers "No — this is for copper/coax only" from product knowledge, closing the sale without Dave needing to respond.

### What Success Looks Like
Dave sells his full tool inventory at 85% of market value over 6 weeks. He never writes a description, never answers a question, and receives funds directly to his bank account. He tells his electrician friends about it.

## Persona 4: Amara — The Immigrant Navigating Low-Trust Markets

**Demographics**: 34-year-old Kenyan-American, arrived in Minneapolis 4 years ago, works as a nurse's aide, sending remittances home, cautious with money

### Goals
- Buy quality secondhand household items as she furnishes her first apartment
- Avoid scams — has been warned repeatedly by community members
- Transact safely without needing to meet strangers alone

### Pain Points
- Has been suspicious of Craigslist since a community member was robbed at a meetup
- English is fluent but nuances of American negotiation culture are unfamiliar (is $200 a fair price? Is asking for $150 rude?)
- Doesn't know the "right" prices for used American furniture and appliances
- Worries about being taken advantage of due to her accent or unfamiliarity with the market

### Behaviors
- Uses WhatsApp primarily; comfortable with mobile
- Trusts word-of-mouth and community recommendations
- Researches pricing carefully before any purchase
- Prefers visible safety features (escrow, verified identities)

### How Her Agent Helps
Amara tells her agent: "I need a kitchen table with 4 chairs for my new apartment. Max $200. Must be clean and sturdy." Her agent finds options, filters to sellers within 8 miles who have verified identities and 10+ completed transactions, and negotiates on her behalf. The agent handles all the cultural complexity of negotiation — Amara never feels rude or out of her depth. Payment goes through escrow; the seller is verified before Amara arranges pickup. If anything goes wrong, the dispute agent mediates.

### What Success Looks Like
Amara furnishes her apartment safely and affordably, with zero anxiety about scams. She begins using AgentBay as her primary secondhand shopping platform and recommends it to her community network.

Pickup scheduled for Saturday, 10 AM." Bob never types a single thing.

## Persona 7: Carlos — The Person Who Hates Negotiating

**Demographics**: 45-year-old accountant in Miami, introspective, conflict-averse, has items to sell but dreads the negotiation process

### Goals
- Sell items without ever having to haggle
- Get a fair price without feeling like he's in a confrontation
- Complete transactions cleanly and move on

### Pain Points
- Has ended up selling items for far below value because lowball offers made him feel uncomfortable saying no
- Dreads the back-and-forth of negotiation; finds it emotionally draining
- Has kept items he wanted to sell simply because he didn't want to deal with negotiation
- Second-guesses himself ("Should I have accepted that offer?")

### Behaviors
- Researches compulsively before any decision
- Prefers automated, clear processes
- Reads reviews extensively before purchases
- Would pay a premium to avoid negotiation friction

### How His Agent Helps
Carlos sets his floor price (minimum he'll accept) and tells his agent: "Don't accept less than $180 for the TV." His agent handles all offers, counters, and negotiations without Carlos seeing any of it. Carlos only gets notified when a deal is done. The agent's negotiation is assertive without being aggressive — Carlos never feels the discomfort of the process.

### What Success Looks Like
Carlos sells all his items at fair prices without a single uncomfortable interaction. He says: "I didn't even know someone bought my stuff until the money showed up."

## Persona 8: Lily — The Person Who Doesn't Know How to Price Items

**Demographics**: 55-year-old librarian in Portland, OR, selling estate items after her mother's passing, emotionally attached to items, uncertain of their value

### Goals
- Not drastically undersell items that might be valuable
- Not waste time on items that have no market value
- Handle the estate process without becoming a full-time resale person

### Pain Points
- Has no idea what antiques, vintage items, or used household goods are worth
- Has accepted lowball offers in the past because she didn't know better
- Can't tell the difference between items worth listing and items worth donating
- Emotional attachment makes it hard to set prices objectively

### Behaviors
- Careful and methodical; reads instructions fully
- Would use an app if she trusted it
- Prefers human-like communication (not robotic interfaces)
- Values being told "why" — wants to understand her agent's reasoning

### How Her Agent Helps
Lily photographs 40 items. Her agent:
- Identifies 12 as worth listing (with estimated sell prices)
- Identifies 28 as better donated (low demand, minimal value)
- For valuable items, explains: "This appears to be a Fiesta ware pitcher from the 1940s. Similar items sell for $35–$65 on eBay. I've listed it for $55."
- Lily can see the pricing rationale for every item

### What Success Looks Like
Lily lists 12 items, sells 9 of them, earns $680, and donates the rest. She never undersells because her agent's pricing is grounded in live market data. She tells her colleague: "It's like having a professional estate sale agent in your pocket."
